Welcome to "Le prelibatezze della prociona"

"The Delicacies of the Raccoon" is my personal collection of recipes and meal planning tools.
As a self-proclaimed raccoon, I'm a true glutton at heart, especially when it comes to desserts and chocolate. But food isn't just about indulgence: it's also about nourishing our bodies and minds.

That is why this website isn't just a collection of my personal recipes; it's also a tool to help you create balanced meal plans tailored to your needs.

Here, you'll find a plant-based focus: a choice driven by compassion for animals, a commitment to personal health, and a responsibility toward our planet. For more on the benefits of plant-based diets, check out the position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics on vegan and vegetarian diets.

I always use alternatives like plant-based milk and butter or nutritional yeast instead of cheese like Parmigiano Reggiano. If you eat meat, you won't find it here — but that doesn't mean you can't enjoy the meal plans or adapt them to your preferences.

Recipe Book

Cooking is my passion — perhaps because it leads to eating, but let's not judge a raccoon! Over the years, I have gathered recipes from countless sources, and now it's time to bring them all together in one place.

How It Works

  • Categorization: Recipes are organized by dish type (protein, carbohydrate, vegetables, dessert, sauce) and dietary labels (vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free).
  • Filtering: Click on a label to see all recipes that match, or combine multiple labels to refine your search.
  • Approval Status: Some recipes are still untested — you'll find a label indicating their status.
  • Search: Use the search bar to find recipes by title, ingredients, or instructions.
  • Portion Adjustments: Each recipe includes ingredient quantities for a standard portion. Adjust the portion size, and the ingredient amounts will update automatically.

Note on Gluten-Free Recipes: Many recipes are labeled as gluten-free, even if they contain flour. If you're celiac or gluten-intolerant, you likely already know how to substitute regular flour with gluten-free alternatives (e.g., rice, chickpea, almond, or nut flour) or find gluten-free versions of bread, pasta, piadina or pizza.

Meal Planner

Planning meals can be a challenge, especially when balancing work, family, and personal time. That's where the meal planner comes in, generating a balanced meal plan for a single day or an entire week, based on your preferences.

How It Works

  • Flexibility: Choose between generating a plan for just one day or the entire week.
  • Dietary Preferences: Filter recipes by dietary labels to match your needs.
  • Ingredient Selection: Randomly generate a plan or select ingredients you already have. You can also choose whether to use only those ingredients or allow for additional grocery shopping.
  • Pre-Selected Staples: Basic ingredients like salt, spices, and flour are pre-selected for convenience, but you can remove them if you don't have them on hand.

Leftovers Option

Enable the include leftovers feature to use dinner as the next day's lunch — perfect for meal prepping or reducing cooking time. Keep in mind that recipes have fixed portions, so the grocery list will reflect those amounts. (Future updates may include portion adjustments based on the number of people.)

Balanced Nutrition

The meal planner ensures nutritional balance:

  • Each day includes at least two vegetable dishes, one carbohydrate, and one protein source.
  • Some recipes are complete meals, fulfilling all nutritional needs on their own.

Notes on Breakfast and Fruit: Fruit isn't included in the meal plans (except in some desserts). After all, picking a fruit is simple enough! Breakfast isn't part of the generated plans, but many desserts can double as breakfast options. Pair them with fruit, yogurt, milk, or tea for a balanced start to your day.

My Smoothie Tip

Lately, I've been starting my day with a nutrient-packed smoothie:

  • Base: Soy milk with chia seeds and cocoa (or vegan chocolate protein powder) for fibres. In particular, chia seeds bring omega-3 and a lot of vitamines.
  • Add-ins: Berries (always in my freezer) or banana, spinach (trust me, you won't taste it unless you overdo it) and date syrup for vitamins and fibres.
  • Healthy Fats: Nuts like almonds or walnuts, and occasionally a spoonful of peanut butter.
